# REVIEW

::: review R01 | ch=3 | Autoimmune gastritis: is it "pangastritis"? | subj=IM | from=GIT Internal Med companion C3-13
A | Book pp. 38, 42 | Autoimmune gastritis affects the fundus and body and **spares the antrum**; "pangastritis" is used only for H. pylori gastritis spreading from antrum to body.
B | GIT Internal Med p. 26 | Autoimmune gastritis affects the body and fundus and is called **"pangastritis"**.
:::

::: review R02 | ch=3 | Misoprostol: a PGE₁ or a PGE₂ analogue? | subj=PHARM | from=GIT Others capsule C3-D1 (ledger PH-15)
A | Book p. 37 | "a synthetic prostaglandin **E₂** analogue".
B | Book p. 48 · GIT Others p. 4 | "Synthetic **PGE1** analog".
note | The book's two pages disagree with each other; GIT Others agrees with p. 48.
:::

::: review R03 | ch=3 | H. pylori triple therapy: duration and first-line status | subj=IM | from=GIT Internal Med companion C3-17
A | Book p. 41 | PPI + amoxicillin + clarithromycin for **14 days**, but **no longer recommended as empirical first-line** unless local clarithromycin resistance is known to be low.
B | Book p. 48 | The same regimen for **10–14 days**.
C | GIT Internal Med p. 31 | The same regimen for **14 days**, "one of the most commonly used" regimens.
note | The book is inconsistent on duration (14 vs 10–14 days), and the sources differ on whether triple therapy is still first-line.
:::

::: review R04 | ch=3 | Levofloxacin dose in the rescue regimen | subj=PHARM | from=GIT Others capsule C3-D2 (ledger PH-16)
A | Book p. 41 | "Levofloxacin **500 mg once daily** + PPI + Amoxicillin".
B | Book p. 48 · GIT Others p. 5 | "levofloxacin **250 or 500 mg twice a day** for 10 days" (with PPI + amoxicillin 1 g twice a day).
note | The book's two pages disagree with each other; GIT Others agrees with p. 48.
:::

::: review R05 | ch=3 | Transfusion threshold in acute upper GI bleeding | subj=IM | from=Integration review of GIT Internal Med companion ledger row GB-08
A | GIT Internal Med p. 39 | Transfuse for shock **or Hb &lt; 10** with recent or active bleeding. The source prints the unit as "mg/dl".
B | Book pp. 50–51 | "IV fluids and blood transfusion **when indicated**"; "Hemoglobin levels are generally a **poor indicator** of the need to transfuse". No numeric threshold is given.
note | For variceal bleeding the book (Ch 23, p. 315) uses a restrictive target of Hb 7–8 g/dL (see R32). The shock criteria from the same source page are taught in Chapter 3; the Hb threshold is not.
:::

::: review R06 | ch=3 | Commonest site of gastric cancer: how common? | subj=PATH | from=GIT Others capsule C3-D3 (ledger TU-13)
A | Book p. 62 | Antrum/pyloric area **60%**, body 15%, fundus 25%.
B | GIT Others p. 15 | Pyloric region **50%**, then **lesser curvature 25%**; it can occur anywhere in the stomach.
note | Both agree that the pyloric (antral) region is the commonest site; the percentages and the second site differ.
:::

::: review R07 | ch=5 | How long is "acute" diarrhoea? | subj=IM | from=GIT Internal Med companion C5-08
A | Book p. 77 | Acute diarrhoea lasts **less than 2 to 3 weeks or, rarely, 6 to 8 weeks**.
B | GIT Internal Med p. 51 | Acute diarrhoea lasts **less than 4 weeks**.
note | Both agree that diarrhoea lasting more than 4 weeks is evaluated as chronic.
:::

::: review R08 | ch=5 | Stool fat and lactose breath test protocols | subj=IM | from=GIT Internal Med companion C5-38
A | Book p. 93 · GIT Internal Med p. 68 (text) | **70–100 g** fat diet with stool collected over **2–3 days**; lactose dose **25 g**.
B | GIT Internal Med pp. 69–70 (tables) | **100 g** fat diet with stool collected over **3 days**; lactose dose **1 g/kg** body weight.
note | The source's text and tables disagree with each other; the book follows the text.
:::

::: review R09 | ch=6 | IBS diagnostic criteria: which Rome edition? | subj=IM | from=GIT Internal Med companion C6-16
A | Book p. 102 (Rome V) | Pain on average **at least 3 days per month** over the last 3 months.
B | GIT Internal Med p. 75 (Rome IV) | Recurrent abdominal pain **at least 1 day per week** in the last 3 months.
note | The 2-of-3 associated features (related to defecation, change in stool frequency, change in stool form) are the same in both.
:::

::: review R10 | ch=6 | Low-FODMAP diet: which foods to limit? | subj=IM | from=GIT Internal Med companion C6-17
A | Book p. 104 | Foods high in fructans and oligosaccharides: **onions, garlic, wheat, rye and legumes**.
B | GIT Internal Med p. 75 | **Potatoes, brown rice, oats and almonds** as examples of foods the patient should limit.
:::

::: review R11 | ch=6 | IBS-C: the second drug option | subj=IM | from=GIT Internal Med companion C6-20
A | Book p. 105 | **Prucalopride** (selective 5-HT4 agonist, prokinetic). Linaclotide appears only in the laxative table on p. 99, for refractory constipation.
B | GIT Internal Med p. 75 | **Linaclotide**, a guanylate cyclase activator that softens stool and stimulates bowel movement.
note | Both give lubiprostone (chloride channel activator) as the first option.
:::

::: review R12 | ch=7 | Widal test: what a high H titre means | subj=MICRO | from=GIT Others capsule C7-D1 (ledger FI-13)
A | Book p. 109 | A high titre to **one** H antigen (≥1:160) suggests recent infection with that type; high O titre **plus more than one** H antigen indicates recent immunisation.
B | GIT Others p. 24 | "**More than one** H antigen (≥1:160) suggest recent infection with this type of Salmonella **and** recent immunization."
note | The GIT Others list here is garbled (its items 1–2 are cut off), so it is not a reliable correction.
:::

::: review R13 | ch=8 | Ulcerative colitis: two rows of GIT Internal Med's p. 76 table vs the book's Fig 8.1 | subj=IM | from=GIT Internal Med companion C8-03 (granulomas); rectum row found in integration review of C8-02
A | Book p. 117 (Fig 8.1) | Granulomas **should not be present** in UC (microgranulomas may accompany ruptured crypt abscesses); GIT Internal Med's own p. 77 table agrees. Rectum: **"typically involved"** in UC.
B | GIT Internal Med p. 76 table | Granulomas in UC: **"occasional"**. Rectum: **"always"** involved in UC (only commonly in Crohn's disease).
:::

::: review R14 | ch=8 | Systemic steroid doses in IBD | subj=IM | from=GIT Internal Med companion C8-17

B | GIT Internal Med p. 80 (drug table) | Methylprednisolone **40–60 mg IV daily**; prednisone **0.25–0.75 mg/kg PO daily**.
:::

::: review R15 | ch=15 | Colorectal cancer: age at presentation | subj=PATH | from=GIT Others capsule C15-D1 (ledger TU-31)

::: review R16 | ch=18 | PBC in the immunoglobulin list: raised AMA or raised IgM? | subj=HEP | from=Hepatology extras ledger A-34
A | Book p. 239 | Under "Serum immunoglobulins": "In primary biliary cirrhosis, **raised IgM**."
B | Hepatology p. 11 | Same list: "In primary biliary cirrhosis, **raised AMA**."
note | Both texts give AMA 95% under autoantibodies.
:::

::: review R17 | ch=18 | What happens to the reabsorbed 10–20% of urobilinogen? | subj=HEP | from=Hepatology extras ledger F-04
A | Book p. 245 | The reabsorbed 10–20% **escape the enterohepatic circulation** and pass to the kidney to be excreted in urine.
B | Hepatology p. 82 | The reabsorbed 10–20% enter the portal blood and are **re-excreted by the liver**; only a small fraction escapes hepatic uptake into urine (figure: ~80% faeces, ~18% enterohepatic, ~2% urine).
:::

::: review R18 | ch=18 | Gilbert's syndrome: decreased uptake or decreased conjugation? | subj=HEP | from=Hepatology extras ledger F-05
A | Book p. 245 (Ch 18) | Gilbert's is listed under "II – Decrease uptake" ("defect of hepatic uptake and/or decrease of conjugation").
B | Book pp. 350–351 (Ch 29) · Hepatology pp. 83–84 | Gilbert's is a **decreased conjugation** disorder (deficient hepatic bilirubin glucuronidation); drugs such as rifampin are the example of decreased uptake.
note | The book's two chapters classify it differently.
:::

::: review R19 | ch=19 | Rest in acute viral hepatitis | subj=HEP | from=Hepatology extras ledger B1-35
A | Book p. 259 (also p. 253 for HAV) | "Normal activity as tolerated"; for HAV, "rest and dietary measures are unhelpful".
B | Hepatology p. 31 | "Bed rest is **mandatory** until the patient is free of jaundice and symptoms and the bilirubin is less than 1.5 mg/dl."
note | The Hepatology source itself also says rest is unhelpful for HAV (p. 24).
:::

::: review R29 | ch=22 | PBC: how many diagnostic criteria are needed? | subj=HEP | from=Hepatology extras ledger C-15

::: review R31 | ch=22 | Alcohol threshold for alcoholic cirrhosis: grams or millilitres? | subj=HEP | from=Hepatology extras ledger C-26

::: review R32 | ch=23 | Transfusion target in acute variceal bleeding | subj=HEP+IM | from=Hepatology extras ledger D-24; the same text is in GIT Internal Med p. 43 (companion row GB-18, out of its scope)
A | Book p. 315 | Transfusion should be **restrictive**, with a target haemoglobin generally around **7–8 g/dL**.
B | Hepatology p. 61 · GIT Internal Med p. 43 | Over-transfusion may cause rebleeding; keep the **haematocrit around 30**.
note | Both warn against over-transfusion; the numeric targets differ.
:::

::: review R33 | ch=23 | Nasogastric (Ryle's) tube in GI bleeding: routine or selective? | subj=HEP+IM | from=Hepatology extras ledger D-25; GIT Internal Med companion GB-09 and GB-25 (context)
A | Book p. 315 (variceal bleeding) | A nasogastric tube may be considered **selectively** but is **not routinely required** in every patient.
B | Hepatology p. 61 (variceal bleeding) | Ryle's tube insertion for aspiration and lavage, listed as part of **routine** resuscitation.
C | GIT Internal Med pp. 40, 46 (non-variceal and lower GI bleeding) | A nasogastric tube for gastric wash and monitoring of ongoing bleeding; nasogastric lavage in the unstable patient with lower GI bleeding.
note | The book's Chapter 3 bleeding pages (pp. 50–53) do not mention a nasogastric tube. The GIT Internal Med uses are kept in Chapter 3 with this cross-reference.
:::

::: review R36 | ch=28 | Terlipressin escalation and albumin dose in HRS | subj=HEP | from=Hepatology extras ledger E-37
A | Book p. 347 · Hepatology text | Terlipressin 1 mg q4–6h or 2 mg/day infusion; escalate if creatinine falls &lt;25% **after 2 days**, to a maximum of 12 mg/day; albumin 20% at **20–40 g/day**.
