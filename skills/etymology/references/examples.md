## 1. Eponym — Bell's palsy

**Bell's palsy — شلل بِل (شلل العصب الوجهي)**

**Naming basis:** Named after Sir Charles Bell (1774–1842), the Scottish surgeon and anatomist who described the function of the facial nerve. "Bell" is a surname, so it is not split into roots.

| Part | Role / origin | English meaning | المعنى بالعربية |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bell's | eponym | of Charles Bell | منسوب إلى تشارلز بِل |
| palsy | Middle English *palesie* ← Old French ← Latin/Greek *paralysis* | paralysis | شلل |

**English definition:** Sudden, usually one-sided weakness or paralysis of the facial muscles caused by dysfunction of the facial nerve, with no identifiable cause.

**التعريف بالعربية:** ضعف أو شلل مفاجئ في عضلات أحد جانبي الوجه نتيجة خلل في العصب الوجهي دون سبب واضح.

**Related word:** *palsy* and *paralysis* are the same word — *palsy* is a worn-down medieval form.

*Why this shape:* no parts table of roots for the surname, a "Naming basis" line instead of "Origin", and the genuinely analyzable word (palsy) still gets explained.

## 2. Acronym — CABG

**CABG — جراحة مَجازة الشريان التاجي**

**Expansion:** **C**oronary **A**rtery **B**ypass **G**raft → طُعْم مَجازة الشريان التاجي.

**Literally:** a graft that carries blood around (bypasses) a coronary artery → «طُعم يُمرِّر الدم حول الشريان التاجي».

**English definition:** Surgery in which a vessel taken from elsewhere in the body is attached so that blood flows around a narrowed or blocked coronary artery.

**التعريف بالعربية:** عملية جراحية يُستخدم فيها وعاء دموي من مكان آخر في الجسم لتمرير الدم متجاوزًا تضيّقًا أو انسدادًا في أحد الشرايين التاجية.

**Note:** often said aloud as "cabbage". Egyptian patients commonly call it «عملية تحويل مسار».

*Why this shape:* the expansion replaces the parts table; each expanded word could be broken down further, but only if the user asks.

## 3. Literal meaning ≠ real meaning — anemia

**Anemia — فقر الدم**

**Origin:** Greek *anaimia* "lack of blood".

| Part | Role / origin | English meaning | المعنى بالعربية |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| an- | prefix; Greek (a- before a vowel) | without, lacking | بدون / انعدام |
| -em- | root; Greek *haima* | blood | دم |
| -ia | suffix; Greek | condition | حالة |

**Literally:** "a condition of having no blood" → «حالة انعدام الدم».

**English definition:** A reduction in haemoglobin concentration or red-cell mass below the normal range for age and sex.

**التعريف بالعربية:** انخفاض تركيز الهيموغلوبين أو كتلة كريات الدم الحمراء عن المعدل الطبيعي بالنسبة للعمر والجنس.

**Important distinction:** the patient is not "bloodless" — blood volume can be normal. The accepted Arabic term فقر الدم ("poverty of blood") is closer to the real meaning than the literal Greek.

*Why this shape:* the mismatch is stated in one sentence, and the established Arabic term is used instead of the literal calque.

## 4. Contested origin — saphenous

**Saphenous (vein) — (الوريد) الصافِن**

**Origin (debated):** Most English dictionaries trace it through Medieval Latin *(vena) saphena* to Arabic *al-ṣāfin* (الصافن), the name used in medieval Arabic medical works. A Greek source, *saphēnēs* "clear, visible", has also been proposed because the vein is often visible under the skin. The ultimate origin is not settled.

**English definition:** Relating to either of the two main superficial veins of the leg (the great and small saphenous veins).

**التعريف بالعربية:** صفة للوريدين السطحيين الرئيسيين في الطرف السفلي (الصافن الكبير والصافن الصغير).

**Note for Arabic speakers:** if the Arabic route is right, the modern Arabic term الوريد الصافن is the word coming home.

*Why this shape:* both views are given with how strong each is, no literal reconstruction is forced, and sources would be cited if a lookup was made.

## 5. Arabic word — صُداع

**صُداع — headache**

**Root and pattern:** root ص د ع (to split, crack) on the pattern فُعال, which Arabic often uses for ailments (سُعال cough, زُكام common cold, دُوار dizziness).

**Literally:** traditionally explained as the pain that feels as if it splits the head → «ألمٌ كأنه يَصدَع الرأس».

**English definition:** Pain felt in the head.

**التعريف بالعربية:** ألم يُحَسّ في الرأس أو أعلى الرقبة.

**Related word:** تَصَدُّع (cracking, e.g. of a wall) shares the same root.

*Why this shape:* the Arabic root-and-pattern system is used instead of a prefix/root/suffix table, and the "traditionally explained" wording keeps the folk explanation from being stated as proven history.
