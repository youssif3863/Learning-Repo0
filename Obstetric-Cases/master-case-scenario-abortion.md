## Discussion {.topic}

::: {.qa}
[Lecturer:]{.qa-l} Summarize the main complaint of this patient?

[Candidate:]{.qa-c} Yes, doctor. The patient is 18 years old, in her first pregnancy, presenting with mild spotting of blood without pain, and her blood sugar is poorly controlled as she is an insulin-dependent diabetic.
:::

::: {.qa}
[Lecturer:]{.qa-l} What is your **provisional diagnosis**?

[Candidate:]{.qa-c} My provisional diagnosis is **threatened abortion**.
:::

::: {.qa}
[Lecturer:]{.qa-l} What are the **points in favor** of this diagnosis?

[Candidate:]{.qa-c}

- She is pregnant with a missed period and positive pregnancy test.
- She has mild vaginal spotting, not associated with pain or passage of clots.
- There are no systemic symptoms like dizziness or shortness of breath.
- No abdominal pain, suggesting the cervix may still be closed.
:::

::: {.qa}
[Lecturer:]{.qa-l} Very good. What are your **differential diagnoses**?

[Candidate:]{.qa-c}

1. **Threatened abortion** – most likely.
2. **Inevitable or incomplete abortion** – if pain or tissue passage occurs.
3. **Ectopic pregnancy** – must be excluded if pain develops or ultrasound shows empty uterus.
4. **Molar pregnancy** – if β-hCG is excessively high or ultrasound shows no viable embryo.
5. **Local cervical cause** – like cervical erosion, polyp, or postcoital bleeding.
:::

::: {.qa}
[Lecturer:]{.qa-l} How will you **confirm the final diagnosis**?

[Candidate:]{.qa-c}

1. **Transvaginal ultrasound** to check for an intrauterine gestational sac, fetal viability, and any subchorionic hematoma.
2. **Serial quantitative β-hCG levels** to assess normal rise of the hormone.
3. **Speculum examination** to ensure the cervix is closed and exclude local lesions.
:::

::: {.qa}
[Lecturer:]{.qa-l} What **investigations** would you request for this patient?

[Candidate:]{.qa-c}

- **Pelvic ultrasound** for fetal viability.
- **CBC** to assess hemoglobin and general condition.
- **Blood sugar profile** (FBS, PPBS, HbA1c) to evaluate diabetic control.
- **Blood group and Rh typing.**
- **Urinalysis** for sugar and infection.
- **β-hCG** quantitative test.
:::

::: {.qa}
[Lecturer:]{.qa-l} How would you **manage** this case if it is threatened abortion?

[Candidate:]{.qa-c}

- **Reassurance and rest:** Advise the patient to rest and avoid heavy work or sexual intercourse temporarily.
- **Control of diabetes:** Strict blood sugar control with adjustment of insulin dose in collaboration with an internal medicine specialist.
- **Supportive therapy:** Folic acid 400 µg daily, and adequate hydration and nutrition.
- **Observation:** Monitor for increase in bleeding or onset of pain.
- **Progesterone support:** May be prescribed if indicated after confirming intrauterine pregnancy.
- **Follow-up:** Repeat ultrasound after one week to assess fetal viability.
:::

::: {.qa}
[Lecturer:]{.qa-l} If the **bleeding increases or pain develops**, what would you suspect?

[Candidate:]{.qa-c} I would suspect the progression to **inevitable or incomplete abortion**, and I would reassess the patient with ultrasound and possibly prepare for uterine evacuation if the miscarriage becomes complete or incomplete.
:::

::: {.qa}
[Lecturer:]{.qa-l} How does **poor diabetic control** affect pregnancy outcome?

[Candidate:]{.qa-c}

- It increases the risk of spontaneous abortion.
- It may cause congenital malformations, especially neural tube and cardiac defects.
- It increases risk of macrosomia, preeclampsia, polyhydramnios, and perinatal complications.
:::

::: {.qa}
[Lecturer:]{.qa-l} what is the **prognosis** in this case?

[Candidate:]{.qa-c}

**If the bleeding remains mild and the fetus is viable with good diabetic control achieved, the prognosis is good and the pregnancy can continue normally.**
**However, if diabetic control remains poor, the risk of miscarriage and fetal anomalies increases significantly.**
:::
