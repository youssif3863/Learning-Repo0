1Reduce acid production Anti-secretory

A. Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) -prazole

Omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole, pantoprazole, rabeprazole (oral / IV).

  • MOA Prodrugs → absorbed → parietal cell canaliculi → activated by acid → irreversibly (covalently) inhibit H⁺/K⁺ ATPase = final step of HCl secretion → most effective acid suppressants (↓ basal & meal-stimulated secretion by 98%).
  • Anti-H. pylori effect (lansoprazole most potent).
  • PK Weak bases destroyed by acid → enteric-coated. Take 30–60 min before meals (inhibit only active pumps; food ↓ bioavailability 50%). t½ = 1 h but action 24 h (new pumps take 24 h to synthesise) → once daily. Liver metabolism.
  • ADRs GIT upset, headache. Hypochlorhydria → infection (hospital pneumonia, H. pylori) & gastric tumours (animals). Vit B12 deficiency on long-term use (↓ intrinsic factor). Enzyme inhibition → ↓ activation of clopidogrel.

B. K⁺-competitive acid blockers (P-CABs) -prazan

Vonoprazan, revaprazan, tegoprazan: newer alternative to PPIs.

C. H2-receptor antagonists -tidine

Cimetidine (prototype), ranitidine (withdrawn: cancer risk), famotidine, nizatidine.

  • MOA Reversible competitive block of H2 on parietal cells → complete block of histamine-mediated, partial block of ACh/gastrin-mediated secretion. Best on nocturnal / fasting secretion.
  • Cimetidine: limited use due to enzyme inhibition, antiandrogenic & CNS effects.
  • Nizatidine = most potent, 100% bioavailability · Famotidine = cytoprotective · ↓ dose in renal/liver disease.
  • ADRs Tolerance & rebound hyperacidity (H2 up-regulation) · CNS confusion (elderly) · antiandrogenic: ↑ prolactin, gynecomastia, galactorrhea, impotence · enzyme inhibition · hypotension & bradycardia with rapid IV (cardiac H2).
PPIs/P-CABs beat H2 blockers: higher efficacy, once-daily dosing (24 h), better in anti-H. pylori regimens.
Indications: peptic ulcer (PPI in severe, H2 in mild) · H. pylori regimens · GERD (H2 covers nocturnal acid) · Zollinger-Ellison (high-dose PPI) · stress ulcer · bleeding varices (IV) · aspiration pneumonia prophylaxis before C-section (IV).

2Protect the lining Mucosal protectives

SucralfateMisoprostolColloidal bismuth
Mech. Al salt of sulfated sucrose. In acid: −ve sulfate binds +ve proteins of ulcer base → barrier. ↑ HCO₃⁻, mucus, PGs. Synthetic PGE1 analogue: ↓ HCl (↓ cAMP), ↑ mucosal blood flow, mucus & HCO₃⁻. Anti-H. pylori (main). Chelates ulcer-base proteins → coat vs acid & pepsin.
Uses Stress-ulcer prophylaxis. Needs acid → give antacids/H2 blockers (and tetracycline) 1–2 h apart. NSAID-induced ulcer if PPIs fail; prophylaxis with NSAIDs/steroids. Quadruple anti-H. pylori therapy.
ADRs Constipation. Al toxicity (renal pts): phosphate chelation → osteomalacia, encephalopathy. Diarrhea & colic (commonest). Uterine contraction → CI in pregnancy (abortion). Black tongue & stools (mimic bleeding). Encephalopathy (renal) → max 2 months.

3Eradicate H. pylori Combination therapy

Eradication → rapid healing & ↓ recurrence. Mnemonic P-CAT: PPI + Clarithromycin + Amoxicillin + Tinidazole/metronidazole.

  • Triple (10–14 d): lansoprazole + clarithromycin + amoxicillin (metronidazole if amoxicillin allergy).
  • Quadruple (10–14 d): PPI + metronidazole 250 mg + bismuth subsalicylate 300 mg + tetracycline 500 mg, four times daily.
  • Sequential: PPI + amoxicillin × 5 d → PPI + clarithromycin + tinidazole/metronidazole × 5 d.
  • Levofloxacin-based: PPI + amoxicillin + levofloxacin × 10 d.
  • Then PPI for 4–6 weeks to heal the ulcer. PPIs can be replaced by P-CABs.

4Neutralise existing acid Antacids

  • Weak bases that neutralise HCl (lasts 1–2 h); pepsin inactive at pH > 4.5; ↑ mucosal PGs; Al/Mg are also adsorbent & demulcent.
  • Taken 1 & 3 h after meals and at bedtime. Uses: rapid pain relief in PU, dyspepsia, GERD.

NaHCO₃
(systemic)		Rapid & potent (pH > 7) but systemic alkalosis, rebound, CO₂ belching, alkaline urine → stones, Na⁺ → salt & water retention (avoid in HF, HTN, renal). Used in effervescent & immediate-release PPI forms.
Choice: Al + Mg combination (e.g. magaldrate): no CO₂, no systemic alkalosis, and they cancel each other's bowel effects. Use antacids cautiously in elderly & renal impairment.
Interactions: change gastric/urine pH, delay gastric emptying, Al³⁺ chelates fluoroquinolones, tetracyclines, digoxin, iron → give 2 h apart.

Exam traps: one line each

  1. Most effective acid suppressant = PPI (final common step).
  2. PPI before meals; P-CAB any time (acid-stable, not a prodrug).
  3. Short t½ but 24 h action = irreversible pump block.
  4. PPI long-term → B12 deficiency, infections; ↓ clopidogrel.
  5. Cimetidine → gynecomastia, enzyme inhibition, confusion.
  6. H2 blockers best for nocturnal acid; rapid IV → bradycardia.
  7. Misoprostol: NSAID ulcer; contraindicated in pregnancy.
  8. Sucralfate needs acid: separate from antacids/H2 by 1–2 h.
  9. Bismuth → black stools that look like GI bleeding.
  10. Al = constipation, Mg = diarrhea → combine them.
  11. CaCO₃ → rebound acid + milk-alkali syndrome.
  12. NaHCO₃ → alkalosis & Na⁺ load (avoid in HF/HTN).
  13. Antacids: 2 h gap from tetracyclines, quinolones, digoxin, iron.
  14. NSAID-ulcer prevention: low dose, no NSAID combos, add PPI or misoprostol, care in elderly.
