Peptic Ulcer Disease: Drug Treatment

High-yield revision · YMnotes Digestive System pp. 44–49 Name clues: PPI -prazole · P-CAB -prazan · H2 blocker -tidine

1Reduce acid productionAnti-secretory drugs

Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) -prazole

Omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole

  • Prodrug, activated by acid → irreversibly blocks H⁺/K⁺ ATPase
  • Blocks the final step of HCl secretion → most effective acid suppressant
  • Enteric-coated; take 30–60 min before meals
  • t½ 1 h but acts 24 h → once daily
  • Lansoprazole: strongest anti-H. pylori effect
  • ADRs: infections (pneumonia), vit B12 deficiency, ↓ activation of clopidogrel

H2-receptor antagonists -tidine

Cimetidine (prototype), famotidine, nizatidine

  • Reversible H2 block on parietal cells
  • Best against nocturnal / fasting acid
  • Cimetidine: enzyme inhibitor, antiandrogenic, CNS effects (gynecomastia, impotence, confusion in elderly)
  • Tolerance & rebound hyperacidity
  • Rapid IV → bradycardia & hypotension
  • Nizatidine = most potent

K⁺-competitive acid blockers (P-CABs) -prazan: vonoprazan

Newer alternative to PPIs: reversible, not a prodrug, acid-stable (take with or without food), full effect in 1 day (PPI 3–5 days).

Uses: peptic ulcer · H. pylori regimens · GERD · Zollinger-Ellison (high-dose PPI) · stress ulcer · before C-section (prevent aspiration). PPIs beat H2 blockers in efficacy and once-daily dosing.

3Eradicate H. pyloriCombination therapy

Eradication = rapid healing & fewer recurrences. Remember P-CAT: PPI + Clarithromycin + Amoxicillin + Tinidazole/metronidazole.

Triple therapy · 10–14 days

  • PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin
  • Amoxicillin allergy → use metronidazole

Quadruple therapy · 10–14 days

  • PPI + bismuth + metronidazole + tetracycline (4× daily)
  • Other options: sequential, levofloxacin-based
Then PPI for 4–6 weeks to heal the ulcer. A P-CAB can replace the PPI.

4Neutralise existing acidAntacids

  • Weak bases that neutralise HCl → rapid but short relief (1–2 h) of ulcer pain, dyspepsia, GERD
  • Taken 1 and 3 h after meals and at bedtime
AntacidKey side effects
Al(OH)₃Constipation · chelates phosphate → osteomalacia, encephalopathy (renal patients)
Mg²⁺ saltsDiarrhea · CNS depression (renal patients)
CaCO₃Rebound hyperacidity · milk-alkali syndrome (alkalosis + hypercalcemia)
NaHCO₃ (systemic)Systemic alkalosis · Na⁺ → salt & water retention (avoid in HF, hypertension)
Best choice: Al + Mg together (e.g. magaldrate): they cancel each other's bowel effects with no systemic alkalosis.
Interaction: Al³⁺ chelates tetracyclines, quinolones, digoxin, iron → give 2 h apart.

Top 10 for the exam

  1. Most effective acid suppressant = PPI
  2. PPI before meals; P-CAB any time
  3. Long-term PPI → B12 deficiency
  4. Cimetidine → gynecomastia, drug interactions
  5. H2 blockers best for night-time acid
  6. Misoprostol: NSAID ulcer, not in pregnancy
  7. Bismuth → black stools
  8. Al = constipation, Mg = diarrhea → combine
  9. CaCO₃ → milk-alkali syndrome
  10. Antacids: 2 h gap from tetracyclines & quinolones
